The main result of this study is not a single random-split score, but an
axis-resolved account of what a defect-energy surrogate learns and where it
fails.  In the validation-locked factorial, gated readout and the composite
pre-normalized, distance-gated local block reduced error consistently, whereas
the independent effect of explicit environment enrichment was inconclusive.
The selected DART recipe outperformed the evaluated SchNet and descriptor
controls across the frozen partitions, although these comparisons do not
isolate a backbone-only advantage.

Chemical transfer within IMP2D depended strongly on the held-out axis.
Host--impurity-pair error remained close to random out-of-fold error, whereas
withholding complete hosts or impurities approximately doubled the MAE.
Nevertheless, pair-held-out predictions retrospectively identified the
lower-energy incorporation class among retained IMP2D candidates with
\PRMPreferenceAccuracy\% accuracy and incurred \PRMGlobalRegret\eV{} mean
global site-selection regret.  Internal uncertainty ranked prediction risk, but
its empirical coverage and interval width do not support conditional,
chemically shifted, or external reliability guarantees.

DART therefore provides a useful, provenance-audited surrogate for
retrospective screening within the neutral IMP2D domain.  Prospective
first-principles validation and transfer beyond that domain remain open tests
rather than claims of the present work.
