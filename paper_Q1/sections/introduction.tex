Point defects and incorporated impurity atoms can alter carrier density,
localized electronic states, magnetism, and catalytic activity in
two-dimensional (2D) materials.  Their formation energies compare a relaxed
defective structure with a pristine host and an atomic reservoir, and therefore
depend on both the local defect environment and the surrounding chemical
reference state~\cite{freysoldt2014}.  Defect structures and energetics can
differ between bulk and monolayer hosts~\cite{komsa2015}, while the accessible
surface and internal voids introduce distinct adsorbate and interstitial
incorporation geometries~\cite{imp2d2023}.  Systematic first-principles surveys
remain demanding because the search space grows across host chemistry,
impurity identity, incorporation class, and symmetry-distinct site.  Once such
a survey exists, a separate question is whether learned representations can
organize its energy landscape and expose where transfer across chemistry or
incorporation geometry fails.

The IMP2D database provides such a survey for neutral adsorbate and
interstitial impurities in 2D hosts~\cite{imp2d2023}, and graph neural
networks are the natural learners because they operate directly on
periodic atomic structures~\cite{alignn2021,schutt2018}.  Defect-specific
models have followed: for bulk energetics through defect graph networks,
semiconductor graph models, persistent-homology features, and
charge-aware predictors~\cite{witman2023,rahman2024,fang2025,kiyohara2025},
and for 2D systems through sparse defect representations on 2DMD and tree
or descriptor models directly on
IMP2D~\cite{kazeev2023,kesorn2024,elalouani2026}.  Together these studies
show that representation and data preparation materially affect reported
errors, and they fix the requirements a model for this task must meet:
operate on the periodic structure \emph{alone}, expose the impurity site
\emph{explicitly}, and respect the \emph{non-extensive}, defect-local
scaling of the target.

Evaluation practice has not kept pace.  A random structure split places
closely related chemistries and numerically equivalent structures on both
sides of the train--test boundary, yielding optimistic
estimates~\cite{li2024mdhit}, and it conflates two physically different
questions: recombining known constituents in an unseen pair versus
predicting a host or impurity absent from all target
supervision~\cite{omee2024,li2025ood}.  One pooled error likewise hides
the role of incorporation geometry --- adsorbate versus interstitial ---
and hides a third failure mode entirely: a representation defect can move
individual predictions by electron-volts while leaving the pooled error
essentially unchanged.

Here we develop the Defect-Aware Radial Transformer (DART), an
impurity-centered graph model that combines defect-local message passing,
radial-bias self-attention, and a gated graph readout matched to a
non-extensive formation-energy target [Fig.~\ref{fig:architecture}], built
on a strictly permutation-invariant local representation with exact
minimum-image distances.  This paper makes four contributions: a chemically resolved
transferability evaluation whose grouped protocols separate random
interpolation, recombination of familiar constituents, a predefined
chemistry block, and transfer to hosts or impurities absent from target
supervision; a preregistered error analysis of \emph{why} the error
structure looks the way it does --- which local environments carry the
error, whether the host-versus-impurity asymmetry reflects structural
novelty or training support, and how the readout's scaling class
interacts with a defect-local target; retrospective reranking metrics
over existing DFT-relaxed candidates, conditional on the completed IMP2D
workflow; and a correctness methodology --- a diagnostic showing that a
superseded, order-sensitive implementation moved individual predictions
by several eV while leaving pooled benchmarks essentially unchanged,
together with the independently audited repair behind every reported
number.  The
architecture factorial that selected the frozen recipe and the internal
uncertainty calibration~\cite{peterson2017,hu2022,grasselli2025} are
reported in the Supplemental Material~\cite{supplemental}.  The paper
proceeds from the audited data set and model (Sec.~\ref{sec:method})
through the frozen evaluation protocol (Sec.~\ref{sec:setup}) to the
results (Sec.~\ref{sec:results}) and their physical reading and limits
(Secs.~\ref{sec:discussion} and~\ref{sec:conclusion}).

\caption{Architecture of the Defect-Aware Radial Transformer (DART).
  (a) Feature initialization of the atomistic graph. A fixed
  128-dimensional ct-UAE vector and nine normalized elemental attributes are
  concatenated and projected from 137 to 128 channels, after which a learned
  binary impurity-status embedding marks the unique impurity node. (b) The
  DART backbone adds four projected defect-environment descriptors only at
  that node before three 5-\AA{} pre-normalized, distance-gated local blocks
  and two four-head geometric Transformer blocks. The radial bias uses 32 basis
  functions spanning 0--12~\AA{}, while attention connects all valid atoms.
  (c) A graph-dependent scalar gate
  fuses an atom-attention weighted sum and a channelwise maximum before a
  $128\rightarrow128\rightarrow1$ head predicts the formation energy of each
  structure. For each host--impurity pair, retrospective incorporation-class reranking
  compares the minimum predicted energy among retained adsorbate candidates
  with the corresponding minimum among interstitial candidates; the displayed
  ordering is illustrative.}
