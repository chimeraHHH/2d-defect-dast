Point defects and incorporated impurity atoms can alter carrier density,
localized electronic states, magnetism, and catalytic activity in
two-dimensional (2D) materials.  Formation energy is therefore a central
quantity in computational defect screening: for a specified host, impurity
reservoir, charge state, and structural configuration, it ranks the energetic
cost of incorporation.  Exhaustive first-principles evaluation remains
expensive because the search space grows across host chemistry, impurity
identity, incorporation class, and symmetry-distinct site.

The IMP2D database provides a systematic first-principles survey of neutral
adsorbate and interstitial impurities in 2D hosts~\cite{imp2d2023}.  Its scale
makes supervised surrogates possible, and graph neural networks are natural
models because they operate directly on periodic atomic structures.  ALIGNN
demonstrated the value of joint bond and angle representations for crystalline
properties~\cite{alignn2021}, while SchNet established continuous-filter
message passing from interatomic distances~\cite{schutt2018}.  Defect-specific
learning has subsequently developed along several complementary directions.
Bulk defect energetics have been treated with defect graph networks,
semiconductor graph models, persistent-homology features, and charge-aware
predictors~\cite{witman2023,rahman2024,fang2025,kiyohara2025}.  For 2D
systems, sparse defect representations have
been evaluated on the 2DMD data, whereas tree and descriptor models have been
applied directly to IMP2D~\cite{kazeev2023,kesorn2024,elalouani2026}.  The two
IMP2D studies show in particular that data representation and model selection
materially affect the reported error.  These developments motivate
architectures that expose the impurity site while retaining both local
coordination and longer-range periodic context.

Existing IMP2D studies establish descriptor baselines and sensitivity to data
engineering, but they do not jointly isolate defect-aware architectural choices
under paired repeats, separate transfer along host, impurity, and
host--impurity axes, and connect out-of-fold energy errors to screening
decisions.

Benchmark accuracy, however, does not by itself define a useful materials
model.  A random structure split can place closely related host--impurity
chemistries, or even numerically equivalent structures, on both sides of the
train--test boundary, a known source of optimistic materials-learning
estimates~\cite{li2024mdhit}.  It also does not answer whether a model transfers
to an unseen host or impurity, whether a reported architectural gain survives
paired repeats, or whether prediction uncertainty identifies a subset on which
screening risk is acceptably low.  Structure-based out-of-distribution studies
have emphasized that materials models can degrade sharply when test chemistry
differs from training chemistry~\cite{omee2024,li2025ood}.  For defect screening,
host and impurity axes must therefore be separated explicitly.
Uncertainty studies in atomistic machine learning have likewise shown that
ensemble disagreement can provide a useful credibility signal, while
calibration and out-of-domain reliability remain distinct
requirements~\cite{peterson2017,hu2022,grasselli2025}.  A defect-screening
surrogate should therefore be judged by its transfer partitions, uncertainty
calibration, and decision-level screening consequences rather than by one
random-split error.

This study tests a single thesis: impurity-energy surrogates should be designed
and evaluated around the localized defect, and their value should be judged by
chemistry-held-out decisions rather than by a random-split score alone.  We ask
which defect-aware modules have reproducible paired effects, how prediction
error changes when a host, impurity, or their combination is absent from IMP2D
target supervision, and whether the resulting out-of-fold predictions remain
useful for screening.

We first reconstruct the IMP2D target provenance, remove structures without a
permutation-invariant impurity identity, control numerical redundancy, and
freeze all evaluation partitions.  We then evaluate DART through a paired
factorial, random and chemistry-grouped cross-validation, and a predefined
host--impurity block.  Architecture and descriptor choices use validation data
only; screening conclusions use pair-held-out out-of-fold predictions; and
uncertainty is evaluated on a separate internal calibration/test split.

Three findings organize the paper.  First, paired evidence supports gated
readout and the composite pre-normalized, distance-gated local block, but not an
independent main effect of explicit environment enrichment.  Second, chemical
transfer is axis dependent: unseen host--impurity pairs remain close to
interpolation, whereas unseen hosts or impurities cause substantially larger
errors.  Third, pair-held-out predictions retain useful decision-level accuracy
and low regret, while internal uncertainty estimates remain too limited to
support universal reliability claims.  All conclusions are restricted to the
audited neutral adsorbate and interstitial evidence in IMP2D.
