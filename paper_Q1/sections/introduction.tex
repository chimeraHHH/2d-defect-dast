Point defects and incorporated impurity atoms can alter carrier density,
localized electronic states, magnetism, and catalytic activity in
two-dimensional (2D) materials.  Their formation energies compare a relaxed
defective structure with a pristine host and an atomic reservoir, and therefore
depend on both the local defect environment and the surrounding chemical
reference state~\cite{freysoldt2014}.  Defect structures and energetics can
differ between bulk and monolayer hosts~\cite{komsa2015}, while the accessible
surface and internal voids introduce distinct adsorbate and interstitial
incorporation geometries~\cite{imp2d2023}.  Systematic first-principles surveys
remain demanding because the search space grows across host chemistry,
impurity identity, incorporation class, and symmetry-distinct site.  Once such
a survey exists, a separate question is whether learned representations can
organize its energy landscape and expose where transfer across chemistry or
incorporation geometry fails.

The IMP2D database provides a systematic first-principles survey of neutral
adsorbate and interstitial impurities in 2D hosts~\cite{imp2d2023}.  Its scale
makes supervised energy estimation possible, and graph neural networks are natural
models because they operate directly on periodic atomic structures.  ALIGNN
demonstrated the value of joint bond and angle representations for crystalline
properties~\cite{alignn2021}, while SchNet established continuous-filter
message passing from interatomic distances~\cite{schutt2018}.  Defect-specific
learning has developed along several complementary directions.
Bulk defect energetics have been treated with defect graph networks,
semiconductor graph models, persistent-homology features, and charge-aware
predictors~\cite{witman2023,rahman2024,fang2025,kiyohara2025}.  For 2D
systems, sparse defect representations have been evaluated on the 2DMD data,
whereas tree and descriptor models have been applied directly to
IMP2D~\cite{kazeev2023,kesorn2024,elalouani2026}.  These studies establish
descriptor baselines for IMP2D and show that representation and data
preparation materially affect reported errors.  They also motivate
architectures that expose the impurity site while retaining local coordination
and longer-range periodic context.  A random
structure split, however, can place closely related host--impurity chemistries
or numerically equivalent structures on both sides of the train--test boundary,
leading to optimistic estimates~\cite{li2024mdhit}.  It also conflates two
physically different questions: combining a known host with a known impurity in
a previously unseen pair, and predicting a host or impurity absent from all
target supervision.  Materials models are known to degrade when test chemistry
differs from training chemistry~\cite{omee2024,li2025ood}, but this distinction
has not been resolved for impurity incorporation energies in IMP2D.  The role of
incorporation geometry---adsorbate versus interstitial---is likewise hidden by
one pooled error.

Here we develop the Defect-Aware Radial Transformer (DART), an
impurity-centered graph model that combines defect-local message passing,
radial-bias self-attention, and graph readout for a non-extensive
formation-energy target [Fig.~\ref{fig:architecture}].  A paired $2^3$
factorial quantifies module effects, after which grouped tests distinguish
transfer to unseen host--impurity pairs from transfer to hosts or impurities
absent from IMP2D target supervision.  Retrospective reranking metrics are
computed from pair-held-out predictions for existing DFT-relaxed candidates.
Because those geometries and their reference energies come from the same
completed DFT workflow, this analysis does not replace structure generation or
relaxation.  A separate ensemble experiment assesses internal uncertainty calibration and selective
prediction~\cite{peterson2017,hu2022,grasselli2025}.

\caption{Architecture of the Defect-Aware Radial Transformer (DART).
  (a) Feature initialization of the atomistic graph. A fixed
  128-dimensional ct-UAE vector and nine normalized elemental attributes are
  concatenated and projected from 137 to 128 channels, after which a learned
  binary impurity-status embedding marks the unique impurity node. (b) The
  DART backbone adds four projected defect-environment descriptors only at
  that node before three 5-\AA{} pre-normalized, distance-gated local blocks
  and two four-head geometric Transformer blocks. The radial bias uses 32 basis
  functions spanning 0--12~\AA{}, while attention connects all valid atoms.
  (c) A graph-dependent scalar gate
  fuses an atom-attention weighted sum and a channelwise maximum before a
  $128\rightarrow128\rightarrow1$ head predicts the formation energy of each
  structure. For each host--impurity pair, retrospective incorporation-class reranking
  compares the minimum predicted energy among retained adsorbate candidates
  with the corresponding minimum among interstitial candidates; the displayed
  ordering is illustrative.}
