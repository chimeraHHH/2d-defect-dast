\subsection{Local and global context shape model choice}
%----------------------------------------------------------------------

The paired factorial separates module effects from variation between data
partitions.  Validation contrasts supported small reductions in MAE for gated
readout (G) and the composite local block (P), but did not resolve an
independent effect of environment enrichment (E).  The negative G:P and
positive E:P interactions indicate nonadditive module effects and help account
for selection of the all-enabled configuration even
though E lacks a resolved main effect.  The four E descriptors may overlap with
information already encoded by local distance and angle messages, but the
factorial does not establish that they are redundant.  Likewise, P changes both
normalization placement and edge gating, so its contrast applies only to the
composite block.

Graph readout provides a direct connection between the prediction target and
model design.  Atomwise addition is appropriate for extensive total energies,
whereas the IMP2D formation energy is a defect excess energy and is
non-extensive with respect to host atom count.  Local coordination is a useful
inductive bias, but the energy difference can retain long-range elastic or
electronic contributions; DART therefore also includes all-atom attention.
Pooling choice is known to affect atomistic prediction of intensive and
localized quantum properties~\cite{buterez2023pooling}.  Replacing only the additive
SchNet readout by mean pooling reduced its host-held-out MAE from
\PRMSchNetAddHostMAE\eV{} to \PRMSchNetMeanHostMAE\eV{} and improved all five
folds.  For this evaluated SchNet recipe, the controlled sensitivity identifies
additive pooling as an important contributor to its large host-held-out error;
it does not establish a universal pooling rule for formation energies.  The remaining
gap to DART (\PRMHostCvDARTMAE\eV{}) reflects the
complete learning recipes, including their inputs, initialization,
interactions, and readouts; it is not evidence for a backbone-only advantage.
Fold-resolved errors and size--error diagnostics are reported in the
Supplemental Material~\cite{supplemental}.

\subsection{Chemical axes and incorporation geometry define applicability}
%----------------------------------------------------------------------

The difference between random and chemistry-grouped evaluation is physically
structured.  Pair-held-out MAE was \PRMPairCvDARTMAE\eV{}, compared with
\PRMIdCvDARTMAE\eV{} under random cross-validation.  In the pair-held-out case,
all but one test structure had both its host and impurity represented elsewhere
in training, so this regime almost entirely tests recombination of familiar
constituent representations.  Withholding every IMP2D
target for a host or impurity instead increased MAE to \PRMHostCvDARTMAE\eV{}
and \PRMDopantCvDARTMAE\eV{}.  This separation shows that pair-held-out
recombination is a less severe transfer problem than an unseen constituent
within this database.

The grouped tests do not represent element- or formula-disjoint pretraining.
The JARVIS source task contains reduced-formula matches for 26 IMP2D host labels
and contains every IMP2D impurity element.  Host- and impurity-held-out
therefore mean absence from IMP2D formation-energy supervision, not absence
from all earlier representation learning.  The results remain internal to the
44 hosts and 65 impurities retained from IMP2D and should not be extrapolated
to arbitrary 2D compounds or reference conventions.

Incorporation geometry creates a second hierarchy.  Interstitial MAE under
pair-held-out prediction was \PRMPairInterstitialMAE\eV{}, more than twice the
\PRMPairAdsorbateMAE\eV{} adsorbate error.  Interstitial sites are typically
more highly coordinated and geometrically confined than adsorbate sites in the
source data~\cite{imp2d2023}, which offers a plausible source of greater
structural sensitivity, but the present analysis does not isolate a mechanism.
The retained population is also selective: the impurity-identity audit removes
316 interstitial and 33 adsorbate structures whose inserted atom cannot be
identified permutation-invariantly.  The observed class asymmetry therefore
applies to the retained population rather than to every released IMP2D row.

\subsection{Retrospective energy estimation and reranking of available relaxed geometries}
%----------------------------------------------------------------------

For a fixed host--impurity pair, the pristine-host energy and impurity chemical
potential in Eq.~\eqref{eq:formation_energy} cancel when sites or incorporation
classes are ranked.  This cancellation helps explain why useful relative
decisions can coexist with a larger absolute formation-energy error.
Pair-held-out predictions identified the lower-energy incorporation class with
\PRMPreferenceAccuracy\% accuracy, and their mean global site-selection regret
was \PRMGlobalRegret\eV{}.  Exact-site accuracy measures recovery of the
reference minimum, whereas regret distinguishes a serious miss from selection
of a nearly degenerate alternative.  The top-two result further quantifies
whether a small follow-up queue retains the reference minimum.

These decisions are conditional on the DFT-relaxed candidate geometries
released by IMP2D.  Because each geometry and its reference energy are outputs
of the same completed DFT workflow, the model does not bypass the relaxation
cost that produced the target.  The analysis therefore concerns retrospective
energy estimation and reranking over available relaxed candidates, not
structural generation, relaxation, thermodynamic populations, kinetics, or
synthesizability.

\subsection{Internal uncertainty and remaining scope}
%----------------------------------------------------------------------

On the dedicated ensemble split, nominal 90\% intervals had
\PRMUQNinetyCoverage\% empirical coverage and mean width
\PRMUQNinetyWidth\eV{}.  Coverage and width must be interpreted together, and
the nonzero oracle gap in Supplemental Fig.~S3~\cite{supplemental} shows that
ensemble disagreement
does not recover all high-error cases.  Because the calibration and test rows
come from the same finite IMP2D population, these results support internal
marginal calibration and risk ranking, not host-conditional,
impurity-conditional, or chemically shifted coverage.

The evidence is limited to neutral adsorbates and interstitials, the source
database's relaxed structures and reference energies, and a model that infers
the reference-energy difference from the impurity-containing graph alone.
Vacancies, substitutions, charged defects, transition levels, migration
barriers, finite-temperature free energies, and competing phases are outside
scope.  Duplicate control removes numerical equivalents detected by the two
declared structural fingerprints but cannot prove that every physical
redundancy has been found.  SchNet and the descriptor families provide aligned
comparators rather than an exhaustive survey of atomistic architectures.

Two implementation details also limit geometric interpretation.  The angle
branch retains the first 32 ordered neighbor pairs at each center, making that
branch sensitive to stored atom order when more pairs are available.  The
global radial bias wraps fractional-coordinate differences componentwise, which
need not select the shortest image in an oblique cell.  The reported results are
reproducible for the released ordering and implementation, but strict
permutation robustness and a true shortest-image global bias have not been
established.

A target-matched external set would provide the most direct next test: its
structures, chemical potentials, convergence settings, and target definition
would need to match the IMP2D convention.  A complementary extension would
calibrate uncertainty directly on host- or impurity-shifted partitions, where
exchangeability is intentionally stressed.
