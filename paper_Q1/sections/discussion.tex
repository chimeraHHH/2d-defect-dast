\subsection{Why chemical transfer is hierarchical}
%----------------------------------------------------------------------

The transfer hierarchy has a structural reading.  Pair-held-out prediction,
in which all but one test structure has both its host and impurity
represented elsewhere in training, costs little over random interpolation
(\PRMRepPairCvMAE{} versus \PRMRepIdCvMAE\eV{}): recombining familiar
constituent representations is nearly free.  The expensive step is a new
constituent, and a new host (\PRMRepHostCvMAE\eV{}) is measurably harder
than a new impurity (\PRMRepDopantCvMAE\eV{}).  The preregistered novelty
analysis attributes this residual asymmetry: the sample-matched error gap
follows the structural novelty of the held-out host --- its composition,
cell, thickness, and coordination environment relative to the training
hosts --- and the association survives controlling for the mechanical
training-support asymmetry between the two protocols, with the same
direction across the fold strata.  A new host changes the structural motif
the defect embeds into, whereas a new impurity largely re-poses a chemistry
seen in other combinations.  Development-era case studies of five named
held-out hosts (Supplemental Material~\cite{supplemental}) trace the same
gradient concretely: hosts with close structural relatives in training
transfer at no measurable cost, while the one host with no structural
relative in the database sits at the failure extreme.

These grouped tests do not represent element- or formula-disjoint
pretraining.  The source task contains reduced-formula matches for 26 IMP2D
host labels and every IMP2D impurity element, so host- and impurity-held-out
mean absence from formation-energy supervision, not absence from all earlier
representation learning.  All conclusions are internal to the retained 44
hosts and 65 impurities --- a set that contains no nitride host --- and to
the IMP2D reference conventions.

\subsection{Why the two incorporation classes fail differently}
%----------------------------------------------------------------------

The class asymmetry (\PRMRepPairInterstitialMAE{} versus
\PRMRepPairAdsorbateMAE\eV{} under pair holdout) is not one phenomenon.
The gated error-map effects separate two regimes.  Interstitial error
concentrates in roomy, loosely constrained cages: it rises with
post-relaxation clearance and with the largest defect-centered neighbor
distance, falls with neighbor-distance dispersion and with covalent-radius
mismatch, and rises with valence mismatch.  A snugly caged impurity sits in
a steep, sterically pinned energy well whose depth follows the local
geometry the model sees directly; a loosely held impurity in a large,
uniform cage samples a flatter landscape with near-degenerate placements,
where the energy depends on subtler electronic contributions.  Adsorbate
error is instead dominated by chemical mismatch, led by signed valence,
with local geometry contributing through the smoothed coordination measure.
These are controlled associations, not causal mechanisms; the sign pattern
is what the frozen analysis established.

How much of the interstitial penalty do these measured descriptors explain?
The preregistered attenuation ladder gives a deliberately conservative
answer: the class gap is firmly established after controls, and adding the
full frozen geometry/chemistry block attenuates the class coefficient by
about 28\% at the point estimate, consistently across all five folds, but
the attenuation interval includes zero, so under the frozen criteria the
penalty is reported as not yet explained by the available descriptors.  The
candidate descriptors capture a real part of the effect; the remainder must
live in physics they do not span, such as the anisotropy of the relaxation
pattern, which the released database does not record.  The retained
population is also selective --- the impurity-identity audit removes 316
interstitial and 33 adsorbate structures --- so the asymmetry applies to
the retained population rather than to every released IMP2D row.

\subsection{Readout and the non-extensive target}
%----------------------------------------------------------------------

The formation energy of Eq.~\eqref{eq:formation_energy} is a defect excess
energy: a difference of extensive totals that does not grow with host atom
count.  Atomwise additive pooling, natural for extensive total energies,
imposes the wrong scaling class on this target.  The complete-recipe
comparison shows the consequence (additive SchNet at
\PRMSchNetAddHostCvMAE\eV{} on withheld hosts), the single-change
sensitivity shows the lever (mean pooling alone recovers to
\PRMSchNetMeanHostCvMAE\eV{}), and the preregistered extensivity diagnostic
adds the residual-level picture: the additive arm's signed residual drifts
by roughly $0.04$\eV{} per atom at the point estimate --- fifty times the
defect-centered model's drift --- although host-level heterogeneity leaves
the host-clustered interval spanning zero, so the mechanism is reported as
suggested but not established at the frozen evidence standard.  Pooling
choice is known to matter for intensive and localized
targets~\cite{buterez2023pooling}; the remaining gap to the defect-centered
model reflects complete recipes, not a backbone-only ranking.
Development-era attribution analyses (Supplemental
Material~\cite{supplemental}) show the same property from inside the
model: occlusion attributions concentrate at the impurity atom and decay
with distance from it, so the learned function is defect-local in the same
sense as the target.

\subsection{Retrospective reranking of available relaxed geometries}
%----------------------------------------------------------------------

For a fixed host--impurity pair, the pristine-host energy and impurity
chemical potential in Eq.~\eqref{eq:formation_energy} cancel when sites or
incorporation classes are ranked, which is why useful relative decisions
coexist with a larger absolute error.  Out-of-fold predictions identified
the lower-energy incorporation class for \PRMRepPreferenceAccuracy\% of
pairs against a \PRMRepPreferenceReference\% class-prior reference, with
\PRMRepWithinRegret\eV{} mean within-class regret.  These decisions are
conditional on the DFT-relaxed candidates released by IMP2D: each geometry
and its reference energy are outputs of the same completed workflow, so no
relaxation cost is bypassed, and the results concern retrospective energy
estimation and reranking --- not structure generation, relaxation,
thermodynamic populations, kinetics, or synthesizability.

\subsection{Correctness, uncertainty, and remaining scope}
%----------------------------------------------------------------------

Every number in this paper comes from a repaired representation whose
permutation invariance and exact minimum-image distances passed an
independent acceptance audit; the source-task pretraining was rebuilt under
the same corrected construction.  The diagnostic of the superseded
implementation is a result in its own right [Fig.~\ref{fig:permutation}]:
sixteen atom-relabeling permutations moved single predictions by up to
$8.8$\eV{} and flipped incorporation-class decisions, while the pooled MAE
moved by less than $10^{-3}$\eV{}.  Aggregate benchmarks are therefore
nearly blind to representation defects that dominate individual predictions
--- a caution we believe generalizes beyond this system.

The evidence remains limited to neutral adsorbates and interstitials, the
source database's relaxed structures and reference energies, and a model
that infers the reference-energy difference from the impurity-containing
graph alone.  Vacancies, substitutions, charged defects, transition levels,
migration barriers, finite-temperature free energies, and competing phases
are outside scope.  Duplicate control removes numerical equivalents
detected by the two declared structural fingerprints but cannot prove that
every physical redundancy has been found.  SchNet and the descriptor
families provide aligned comparators under the frozen protocol rather than
an exhaustive survey: a development-era survey (Supplemental
Material~\cite{supplemental}) additionally evaluated trained equivariant
models (MACE, ViSNet), ALIGNN, and the MACE-MP-0 foundation model as a
zero-shot label surrogate --- the latter essentially uncorrelated with the
IMP2D formation-energy convention --- and none approached the
defect-centered design at matched scale, consistent with recent reports
that universal interatomic potentials degrade precisely on interstitial-like
environments far from their bulk training distributions.  In absolute
terms, the repaired random-split and pair-held-out errors sit in the same
few-tenths-of-an-eV band reported for specialized defect formation-energy
models on bulk and low-dimensional
benchmarks~\cite{witman2023,kiyohara2025,kazeev2023}; the grouped regimes
report the additional cost of chemical novelty that pooled numbers do not
expose.  Internal ensemble uncertainty (Supplemental
Material~\cite{supplemental}) supports marginal calibration and risk
ranking within the retained population, not host-conditional or chemically
shifted coverage.  The single-database scope is itself an evidence-based
protocol choice: development-era experiments (Supplemental
Material~\cite{supplemental}) found zero-shot cross-database evaluation on
JARVIS vacancy data failing at the 2--3\,eV level with negative $R^2$,
few-shot fine-tuning losing to training from scratch, and multi-source
joint training buying small in-distribution gains at the price of degraded
held-out-host transfer --- the barrier is one of reference
conventions and defect-class mismatch, which is exactly what a
target-matched external set --- structures, chemical potentials,
convergence settings, and target definition matching the IMP2D convention,
generated independently of their reference energies --- would address as
the most direct next test.  A development-era pilot (Supplemental
Material~\cite{supplemental}) has already exercised that test end to end,
running independent plane-wave calculations on model-ranked candidates: it
located the barrier in per-element reference conventions --- rank agreement
survives once a per-element offset is removed --- and confirmed that model
ranking concentrates first-principles effort well above the pool base
rate, so the remaining step is convention matching rather than new
machinery.
