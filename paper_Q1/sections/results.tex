\subsection{Effects of defect-aware model components}
\label{sec:factorial_results}
%----------------------------------------------------------------------

Validation MAE selected the all-enabled \PRMSelectedVariant{} variant.  Paired
contrasts resolved lower errors for gated readout and the composite
pre-normalized, distance-gated local block, whereas the independent main effect
of environment enrichment was inconclusive.  The complete factorial is
reported in Table~\ref{tab:factorial}, with its paired-effect visualization in
Supplemental Fig.~S2~\cite{supplemental}.  The selected
variant had mean validation MAE
\PRMSelectedValidationMAE\eV{} (95\% bootstrap interval
[\PRMSelectedValidationMAELow, \PRMSelectedValidationMAEHigh]\eV{}) across
the five paired repeats and test MAE
\PRMSelectedTestMAE\eV{} [\PRMSelectedTestMAELow,
\PRMSelectedTestMAEHigh]\eV{}.

\subsection{Interpolation, chemical transfer, and low-energy behavior}
\label{sec:transfer_results}
%----------------------------------------------------------------------

Chemical transfer was strongly axis dependent.  Mean absolute error was
\PRMPairCvDARTMAE\eV{} when complete host--impurity pairs were withheld, versus
\PRMIdCvDARTMAE\eV{} under random cross-validation.  Withholding complete hosts
or impurities increased MAE to \PRMHostCvDARTMAE\eV{} and
\PRMDopantCvDARTMAE\eV{}, respectively.  The predefined chemistry block had MAE
\PRMChemistryBlockDARTMAE\eV{} but samples a different target distribution and
should therefore be interpreted separately rather than placed on a monotonic
extrapolation scale.  Across all five regimes, paired absolute-error differences
favored DART over both the descriptor model selected by validation MAE within
each split and the additive-readout SchNet baseline.
Figure~\ref{fig:transfer} places the five transfer regimes and their paired
comparator contrasts on a common visual scale; Table~\ref{tab:benchmark}
reports the complete intervals.

Aggregate MAE can hide failures in chemically sparse or reranking-relevant
subsets.  Table~\ref{tab:applicability} therefore reports equal-weight host and
impurity macro errors together with performance on nonpositive formation
energies and the lowest-energy decile.  For example, on pair-held-out
predictions, the true low-decile MAE was
\PRMPairCvDARTLowEnergyMAE\eV{} and its recall by the predicted low decile was
\PRMPairCvDARTLowEnergyRecall\%.  None of these low-energy summaries entered
architecture or hyperparameter selection.

Incorporation geometry introduced an additional asymmetry.  Under

\subsection{Internal uncertainty and selective prediction}
\label{sec:uq_results}
%----------------------------------------------------------------------

On the internal ensemble test partition, the five-member ensemble had MAE
\PRMUQTestMAE\eV{}, Gaussian negative log
likelihood \PRMUQNLL, and mean Gaussian CRPS \PRMUQCRPS\eV{}.  The Spearman
correlation between calibrated standard deviation and absolute error was
\PRMUQErrorSpearman.  The risk--coverage curve had AURC \PRMUQAURC\eV{} and
excess AURC \PRMUQExcessAURC\eV{} relative to oracle error ordering.

At 90\% nominal coverage, the split-conformal interval covered
\PRMUQNinetyCoverage\% of test structures with mean width
\PRMUQNinetyWidth\eV{}.  Supplemental Table~SII and
Fig.~S3~\cite{supplemental} report all evaluated nominal levels, the
risk--coverage curve, and the error--uncertainty relation.  These values measure
empirical marginal coverage within the internal ensemble split, not
calibration under a chemically shifted distribution.

\subsection{Retrospective out-of-fold reranking within IMP2D}
\label{sec:reranking_results}
%----------------------------------------------------------------------

The host--impurity-pair folds provide one out-of-fold prediction for every
retained structure.  Both the input geometries and reference energies come
from the completed IMP2D DFT workflow, so the following comparisons are
retrospective rather than a substitute for relaxation.  Across pairs
containing both incorporation classes, DART
identified the lower-energy class among the retained, DFT-relaxed IMP2D
candidates with
\PRMPreferenceAccuracy\% accuracy; the predicted and reference class margins
had Spearman correlation \PRMPreferenceMarginSpearman{} and margin MAE
\PRMPreferenceMarginMAE\eV{}.  Selecting one configuration globally gave
\PRMGlobalExactAccuracy\% exact-site accuracy and mean regret
\PRMGlobalRegret\eV{}.  When the incorporation class was fixed, exact and
top-two site accuracies were \PRMWithinTypeExactAccuracy\% and
\PRMWithinTypeTopTwoAccuracy\%, respectively, with mean regret
\PRMWithinTypeRegret\eV{}.
The corresponding descriptive reference accuracies were
\PRMPreferenceReferenceAccuracy\%, \PRMGlobalReferenceAccuracy\%,
\PRMWithinTypeReferenceExactAccuracy\%, and
\PRMWithinTypeReferenceTopTwoAccuracy\%, respectively.  Relative to these
references, DART improved accuracy by
\PRMPreferenceGain{} [\PRMPreferenceGainLow, \PRMPreferenceGainHigh],
\PRMGlobalAccuracyGain{} [\PRMGlobalAccuracyGainLow,
\PRMGlobalAccuracyGainHigh], \PRMWithinTypeExactAccuracyGain{}
[\PRMWithinTypeExactAccuracyGainLow, \PRMWithinTypeExactAccuracyGainHigh], and
\PRMWithinTypeTopTwoAccuracyGain{} [\PRMWithinTypeTopTwoAccuracyGainLow,
\PRMWithinTypeTopTwoAccuracyGainHigh] percentage points, respectively.

Complete decision metrics and clustered intervals are reported in
Supplemental Table~SIII~\cite{supplemental}.  Figure~\ref{fig:reranking}
shows the class-margin agreement, the distribution of reranking regret, and
the clustered decision-accuracy intervals.
