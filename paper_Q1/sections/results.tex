\subsection{Chemical transfer is hierarchical, and the hierarchy tracks
structural novelty}
\label{sec:transfer_results}
%----------------------------------------------------------------------

Withholding chemistry degrades prediction in a strict order.  With the
repaired graph representation, mean absolute error is
\PRMRepIdCvMAE\eV{} under random cross-validation, rises modestly to
\PRMRepPairCvMAE\eV{} when complete host--impurity \emph{pairs} are
withheld and to \PRMRepChemBlockMAE\eV{} on the predefined host-by-impurity
chemistry block, but jumps to \PRMRepDopantCvMAE\eV{} when complete
impurities are withheld and to \PRMRepHostCvMAE\eV{} when complete hosts
are withheld.  Recombining constituents that were each seen in training is
therefore only mildly harder than interpolation, whereas encountering a new
constituent roughly doubles the error, and a new host is the hardest axis
of all.  Figure~\ref{fig:transfer} places the five regimes and the two
aligned comparators --- the additive-readout SchNet under the identical
protocol and the descriptor model selected by validation MAE within each
split --- on a common scale, and Table~\ref{tab:benchmark} reports the
values; the defect-centered model leads every regime, and the comparator
gaps widen precisely on the constituent-transfer axes.

($p=\PRMPThreeGapP$).  To ask \emph{why} host holdout is harder, we

valence, and period/group --- together with a training-support covariate that
captures the mechanical asymmetry between the two protocols.

\subsection{Interstitial error is a local-crowding effect; adsorbate error is
a chemical-mismatch effect}
\label{sec:error_map_results}
%----------------------------------------------------------------------

Under pair-held-out prediction the two incorporation classes fail for
different, measurable reasons.  Fitting the out-of-fold error against frozen
local geometry and chemistry descriptors --- with host, impurity, and fold
absorbed as crossed effects, and every contrast preregistered before any
outcome was viewed --- yields seven associations that pass the main-text
gate (adjusted decile contrast of at least $0.10\,$eV or 20\%, clustered
interval excluding zero, direction stable in at least four of five folds,
and surviving the exclusion of pathological out-of-plane reconstructions).

For \emph{adsorbates}, error tracks chemistry: moving from the tenth to the
ninetieth percentile of signed valence mismatch raises the adjusted error by
\PRMPOneAdsValenceContrast\eV{}, and the smooth coordination number
contributes \PRMPOneAdsCoordContrast\eV{}
(+\PRMPOneAdsCoordPercent\%).  For \emph{interstitials}, error tracks the
local cage: post-relaxation minimum clearance
(\PRMPOneIntClearanceContrast\eV{}), neighbor-distance dispersion
(\PRMPOneIntDispersionContrast\eV{}), the effective maximum neighbor
distance seen by the defect-centered module
(\PRMPOneIntMaxDistContrast\eV{}), absolute valence mismatch
(\PRMPOneIntAbsValenceContrast\eV{}), and absolute covalent-radius mismatch
(\PRMPOneIntRadiusContrast\eV{}) all carry gated effects.
Figure~\ref{fig:error_map} reports the four-panel physical error map:
class-resolved adjusted error profiles over coordination and clearance,
the chemistry-mismatch contrasts, error against the out-of-plane expansion
factor, and host-family and impurity-series macro errors.

The asymmetry is large in absolute terms: under pair-held-out prediction,
interstitial structures have MAE \PRMRepPairInterstitialMAE\eV{} against
\PRMRepPairAdsorbateMAE\eV{} for adsorbates --- a factor of $2.2$.

To quantify how much of the interstitial penalty the measured environment
explains, we compare the incorporation-class coefficient before and after
adding the full geometry/chemistry block to a controls-only model.

\subsection{Formation energy is not atomwise additive, and the readout must
respect that}
\label{sec:readout_results}
%----------------------------------------------------------------------

The impurity formation energy is a difference of extensive totals and is
therefore a \emph{local} target: its magnitude does not grow with supercell
size.  A sum readout imposes extensive scaling on the prediction, and the
data show the consequence directly.  Under the same training protocol and
host-held-out splits, the additive-readout SchNet baseline reaches a pooled
MAE of \PRMSchNetAddHostCvMAE\eV{}; replacing only its readout by a mean
reduces this to \PRMSchNetMeanHostCvMAE\eV{}, still well above the
defect-centered model.  The preregistered extensivity diagnostic makes the
mechanism explicit:
\PRMWOneAddSlope{}%

readout sensitivity is a diagnostic, not a universal backbone ranking.

\subsection{Retrospective out-of-fold reranking within IMP2D}
\label{sec:reranking_results}
%----------------------------------------------------------------------

The host--impurity-pair folds provide one out-of-fold prediction for every
retained structure.  Both the input geometries and reference energies come
from the completed IMP2D DFT workflow, so the following comparisons are
retrospective rather than a substitute for site generation or relaxation.
Across pairs containing both incorporation classes, the repaired model
identifies the lower-energy class among the retained, DFT-relaxed IMP2D
candidates with \PRMRepPreferenceAccuracy\%\ accuracy
[\PRMRepPreferenceAccuracyLow, \PRMRepPreferenceAccuracyHigh], against an
empirical majority-class reference of \PRMRepPreferenceReference\%\ ---
a gain of \PRMRepPreferenceGain{} percentage points
[\PRMRepPreferenceGainLow, \PRMRepPreferenceGainHigh] --- and the predicted
and reference class margins have Spearman correlation
\PRMRepMarginSpearman.  With the incorporation class fixed, exact-site and
top-two accuracies are \PRMRepWithinExactAccuracy\%\ and
\PRMRepWithinTopTwoAccuracy\%\ against references of
\PRMRepWithinExactReference\%\ and \PRMRepWithinTopTwoReference\%,
with mean reranking regret \PRMRepWithinRegret\eV{}.  Complete decision
metrics with pair-clustered intervals are reported in the Supplemental
Material~\cite{supplemental}.  Figure~\ref{fig:reranking} shows the
class-margin agreement, the regret distributions, and the clustered
decision-accuracy intervals; class flips concentrate where the reference
margin itself is small.
