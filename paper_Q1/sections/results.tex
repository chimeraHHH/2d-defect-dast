\subsection{Validation-locked architecture selection}
\label{sec:factorial_results}
%----------------------------------------------------------------------

The validation-only rule selected the all-enabled \PRMSelectedVariant{} variant.
Paired contrasts consistently favored gated readout and the composite
pre-normalized, distance-gated local block, whereas the independent main effect
of environment enrichment was inconclusive.  Selection of the all-enabled
variant therefore does not imply that every enabled module was independently
beneficial.  The complete paired factorial is reported in
Table~\ref{tab:factorial} and Fig.~\ref{fig:factorial}.  Its selected validation
MAE had a mean of
\PRMSelectedValidationMAE\eV{} (95\% bootstrap interval
[\PRMSelectedValidationMAELow, \PRMSelectedValidationMAEHigh]\eV{}) across
the five paired repeats.  Its locked test MAE was
\PRMSelectedTestMAE\eV{} [\PRMSelectedTestMAELow,
\PRMSelectedTestMAEHigh]\eV{}.  The test values were exposed only after the
selection rule had fixed the promoted architecture.

from variation between random partitions.  Because P jointly changes
normalization placement and distance gating, its contrast supports or rejects
that composite block only; it does not identify either subchange as the causal
source of an effect.

\subsection{Interpolation, chemical transfer, and low-energy behavior}
\label{sec:transfer_results}
%----------------------------------------------------------------------

Chemical transfer was strongly axis dependent.  Pair-held-out MAE
(\PRMPairCvDARTMAE\eV{}) remained close to random out-of-fold MAE
(\PRMIdCvDARTMAE\eV{}), whereas withholding complete hosts or impurities
increased MAE to \PRMHostCvDARTMAE{} and \PRMDopantCvDARTMAE\eV{},
respectively.  The predefined chemistry block had MAE
\PRMChemistryBlockDARTMAE\eV{} but samples a different target distribution and
is not a monotonic measure of extrapolation difficulty.  Across all five
regimes, paired absolute-error differences favored the selected DART recipe over
the prespecified additive-readout periodic SchNet recipe and the split-local
validation-selected descriptor controls.  Table~\ref{tab:benchmark} reports the
complete intervals.

Aggregate MAE can hide failures in chemically sparse or screening-relevant
subsets.  Table~\ref{tab:applicability} therefore reports equal-weight host and
impurity macro errors together with performance on nonpositive formation
energies and the lowest-energy decile.  For example, on pair-held-out
predictions, the true low-decile MAE was
\PRMPairCvDARTLowEnergyMAE\eV{} and its recall by the predicted low decile was
\PRMPairCvDARTLowEnergyRecall\%.  None of these low-energy summaries entered
architecture or hyperparameter selection.

\subsection{Internal uncertainty and selective prediction}
\label{sec:uq_results}
%----------------------------------------------------------------------

On the dedicated uncertainty test partition, which was excluded from ensemble
fitting, checkpoint selection, and calibration but not from earlier
architecture development on other partitions of the same corpus, the
five-member ensemble had MAE \PRMUQTestMAE\eV{}, Gaussian negative log
likelihood \PRMUQNLL, and mean Gaussian CRPS \PRMUQCRPS\eV{}.  The Spearman
correlation between calibrated standard deviation and absolute error was
\PRMUQErrorSpearman.  The risk--coverage curve had AURC \PRMUQAURC\eV{} and
excess AURC \PRMUQExcessAURC\eV{} relative to oracle error ordering.

\input{generated/tab_uq.tex}

At 90\% nominal coverage, the split-conformal interval covered
\PRMUQNinetyCoverage\% of test structures with mean width
\PRMUQNinetyWidth\eV{}.  Table~\ref{tab:uq} reports all prespecified nominal
levels.  These are internal empirical marginal-coverage results; they do not
establish conditional coverage for every host or impurity or performance on a
fully untouched external corpus.

\subsection{Out-of-fold screening decisions within IMP2D}
\label{sec:screening_results}
%----------------------------------------------------------------------

The host--impurity-pair folds provide one out-of-fold prediction for every
retained structure.  Across pairs containing both incorporation classes, DART
retrospectively identified the lower-energy class among the retained IMP2D
candidates with
\PRMPreferenceAccuracy\% accuracy; the predicted and reference class margins
had Spearman correlation \PRMPreferenceMarginSpearman{} and margin MAE
\PRMPreferenceMarginMAE\eV{}.  Selecting one configuration globally gave
\PRMGlobalExactAccuracy\% exact-site accuracy and mean regret
\PRMGlobalRegret\eV{}.  When the incorporation class was fixed, exact and
top-two site accuracies were \PRMWithinTypeExactAccuracy\% and
\PRMWithinTypeTopTwoAccuracy\%, respectively, with mean regret
\PRMWithinTypeRegret\eV{}.
The corresponding non-trained empirical-majority or candidate-uniform reference
accuracies were \PRMPreferenceReferenceAccuracy\%,
\PRMGlobalReferenceAccuracy\%, \PRMWithinTypeReferenceExactAccuracy\%, and
\PRMWithinTypeReferenceTopTwoAccuracy\%.  Model-minus-reference gains were
\PRMPreferenceGain{} [\PRMPreferenceGainLow, \PRMPreferenceGainHigh],
\PRMGlobalAccuracyGain{} [\PRMGlobalAccuracyGainLow,
\PRMGlobalAccuracyGainHigh], \PRMWithinTypeExactAccuracyGain{}
[\PRMWithinTypeExactAccuracyGainLow, \PRMWithinTypeExactAccuracyGainHigh], and
\PRMWithinTypeTopTwoAccuracyGain{} [\PRMWithinTypeTopTwoAccuracyGainLow,
\PRMWithinTypeTopTwoAccuracyGainHigh] percentage points, respectively.
