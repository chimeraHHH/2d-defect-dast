\title{Defect-aware graph transformer for impurity formation energies in
two-dimensional materials: Chemical transferability and error analysis}

\author{Yiming Hua}
\affiliation{Wuhan University, Wuhan, China}
% Author-controlled submission blocker: insert the verified full author list,
% affiliations, corresponding-author email, and ORCIDs.

\date{\today}

\begin{abstract}
\ifprmresultsready
Graph models now predict defect formation energies at useful accuracy, but
pooled benchmarks answer neither \emph{where} such models transfer across
chemistry nor \emph{why} they fail where they do.  We address both
questions for neutral impurities in two-dimensional materials with the
Defect-Aware Radial Transformer (DART), an impurity-centered graph model
built on a strictly permutation-invariant local representation with exact
minimum-image distances, evaluated on 10,224 structures curated from the
IMP2D database.  Every geometry and reference energy is a product of the
same completed DFT workflow, so the task is retrospective,
database-conditioned estimation and reranking rather than a replacement
for candidate generation or relaxation.  Transfer is organized by chemical
novelty in a strict hierarchy: mean absolute error grows only mildly from
random cross-validation (\PRMRepIdCvMAE\eV{}) through withheld
host--impurity pairs (\PRMRepPairCvMAE\eV{}) and a predefined chemistry
block (\PRMRepChemBlockMAE\eV{}), but roughly doubles when a complete
impurity (\PRMRepDopantCvMAE\eV{}) or host (\PRMRepHostCvMAE\eV{}) is
withheld, and the host--impurity asymmetry tracks the structural novelty
of the held-out host after controlling for training support.
Incorporation geometry forms a second axis with distinct physics:
interstitial structures (\PRMRepPairInterstitialMAE\eV{}) are over twice
as hard as adsorbates (\PRMRepPairAdsorbateMAE\eV{}), and preregistered
error maps place interstitial error in roomy, loosely constrained cages
while adsorbate error tracks chemical mismatch, led by signed valence.
The formation energy's non-extensive, defect-local character constrains
model design: an additive readout degrades to \PRMSchNetAddHostCvMAE\eV{}
on withheld hosts, a mean readout alone recovers to
\PRMSchNetMeanHostCvMAE\eV{}, and the defect-centered readout shows no
size-dependent residual drift.  Out-of-fold predictions select the
lower-energy incorporation class for \PRMRepPreferenceAccuracy\% of
host--impurity pairs against a \PRMRepPreferenceReference\% class-prior
reference, with \PRMRepWithinRegret\eV{} mean within-class regret.  All
numbers derive from an independently audited repaired representation:
under atom relabeling, the superseded implementation moved single
predictions by up to several eV while pooled errors changed by under
$10^{-3}$\,eV --- aggregate benchmarks can hide representation defects
that dominate individual predictions.
\else
We study how impurity formation-energy prediction changes across host,
impurity, and incorporation-geometry axes in two-dimensional materials.  The
analysis uses a defect-aware graph Transformer and a curated subset
of IMP2D; numerical results are available only when the corresponding audited
result bundle is present.
\fi
\end{abstract}
