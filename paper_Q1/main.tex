\title{DART: Defect-Aware Radial Transformer with Physically Informed
       Innovations for Formation Energy Prediction in Two-Dimensional Materials}

\author{Yiming Hua}
\affiliation{Wuhan University, Wuhan, China}
% TODO: add other authors

\date{\today}

\begin{abstract}
Point defects govern the electronic, optical, and catalytic properties of
two-dimensional (2D) materials, yet first-principles formation-energy
calculations remain prohibitively expensive for exhaustive host--dopant
screening.  Here we present \dart{} (Defect-Aware Radial Transformer), a
compact graph transformer that combines local bond-angle message passing,
global periodic self-attention, and three targeted architectural
innovations---defect-aware gated attention pooling, local environment
enrichment, and pre-norm residual connections---to predict defect formation
energies on the IMP2D benchmark with substantially fewer parameters than
existing approaches.  A small ensemble of physically diverse models further
improves accuracy beyond a much larger prior ensemble.

We systematically evaluate twelve physically motivated innovations spanning
architecture, loss functions, and training strategies, using bootstrap
hypothesis testing with rigorous statistical controls.  Notably, all
auxiliary-loss and reweighting strategies significantly \emph{worsen}
performance, while targeted architectural changes provide consistent gains---a
finding that challenges the common practice of importing imbalanced-learning
techniques from computer vision into data-limited materials science.

A graduated three-tier out-of-distribution protocol quantifies deployment
boundaries from interpolation through intra-family transfer to compositional
extrapolation, revealing graceful degradation.  Temperature-scaled ensemble
uncertainty provides well-calibrated confidence estimates.  Together, these
results establish \dart{} as an efficient and rigorously validated surrogate
for accelerating defect engineering in 2D materials.
\end{abstract}
