\title{Learning impurity incorporation energetics across host and impurity
chemistry in two-dimensional materials with a defect-aware graph Transformer}

\author{Yiming Hua}
\affiliation{Wuhan University, Wuhan, China}
% Author-controlled submission blocker: insert the verified full author list,

\date{\today}

\begin{abstract}
\ifprmresultsready
We study retrospective estimation and reranking of impurity formation energies
for available DFT-relaxed candidate geometries in two-dimensional materials.
In IMP2D, each relaxed geometry and its reference energy are products of the
same completed DFT workflow; this task therefore evaluates
database-conditioned prediction rather than replacing candidate generation or
relaxation.  Random structure splits nevertheless conceal how errors change
with chemical novelty.  We develop the Defect-Aware Radial Transformer
(DART) and evaluate it on a curated set of 10,224 neutral structures from the
Impurities in Two-Dimensional Materials (IMP2D) database.  Across five paired
repeats, a $2^3$ factorial showed small error reductions from gated graph
readout and the pre-normalized, distance-gated local block, whereas the
independent effect of explicit environment enrichment remained inconclusive.
For pair-held-out structures, all but one test structure had both its host and
impurity represented elsewhere in training; the mean absolute error was
\PRMPairCvDARTMAE\eV{}, compared with \PRMIdCvDARTMAE\eV{} under random
cross-validation.  Withholding every structure
of a host or impurity increased the error to \PRMHostCvDARTMAE\eV{} and
\PRMDopantCvDARTMAE\eV{}, respectively.  Incorporation geometry formed another
applicability axis: pair-held-out interstitial structures had
\PRMPairInterstitialMAE\eV{} error, more than twice the
\PRMPairAdsorbateMAE\eV{} error for adsorbates.  For candidates sharing a host
and impurity, pair-held-out predictions selected the lower-energy incorporation
class with \PRMPreferenceAccuracy\% accuracy and
\PRMGlobalRegret\eV{} mean site-selection regret.  Thus, within IMP2D,
pair-held-out transfer almost entirely recombines familiar constituents and is
substantially easier than transfer to a new constituent, while interstitial
incorporation remains the more difficult energy-prediction regime.
\else
We study how impurity formation-energy prediction changes across host,
impurity, and incorporation-geometry axes in two-dimensional materials.  The
analysis uses a defect-aware graph Transformer and a curated subset
of IMP2D; numerical results are available only when the corresponding audited
result bundle is present.
\fi
\end{abstract}
