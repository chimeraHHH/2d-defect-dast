\title{Defect-aware graph transformer for impurity formation energies in
two-dimensional materials: Chemical transferability and error analysis}

\author{Yiming Hua}
\affiliation{Wuhan University, Wuhan, China}
% Author-controlled submission blocker: insert the verified full author list,
% affiliations, corresponding-author email, and ORCIDs.

\date{\today}

\begin{abstract}
\ifprmresultsready
Graph models now predict defect formation energies at useful accuracy, but
pooled benchmarks answer neither \emph{where} such models transfer across
chemistry nor \emph{why} they fail where they do.  We address both
questions for neutral impurities in two-dimensional materials with the
Defect-Aware Radial Transformer (DART), an impurity-centered graph model
built on a strictly permutation-invariant local representation with exact
minimum-image distances and evaluated on 10,224 structures curated from
the IMP2D database; because every geometry and reference energy comes
from the same completed DFT workflow, the task is retrospective,
database-conditioned estimation and reranking.  Transfer is organized by
chemical novelty in a strict hierarchy: recombining constituents seen in
training costs little over random interpolation, whereas withholding a
complete impurity or host roughly doubles the error, and the
host--impurity asymmetry tracks the structural novelty of the held-out
host after controlling for training support.  Incorporation geometry
forms a second axis with distinct physics: interstitials are over twice
as hard as adsorbates, with preregistered error maps placing interstitial
error in roomy, loosely constrained cages and adsorbate error on chemical
mismatch, led by signed valence.  The formation energy's non-extensive,
defect-local character constrains model design: an additive readout
degrades sharply on withheld hosts, a mean readout alone recovers most of
that gap, and the defect-centered readout shows no size-dependent drift.
Out-of-fold predictions identify the lower-energy incorporation class
well above the class-prior reference, supporting retrospective reranking
within the retained neutral domain.  Every reported number derives from
an independently audited repaired representation: under atom relabeling,
a superseded implementation moved single predictions by several
electron-volts while pooled errors were essentially unchanged ---
aggregate benchmarks can hide representation defects that dominate
individual predictions.
\else
We study how impurity formation-energy prediction changes across host,
impurity, and incorporation-geometry axes in two-dimensional materials.  The
analysis uses a defect-aware graph Transformer and a curated subset
of IMP2D; numerical results are available only when the corresponding audited
result bundle is present.
\fi
\end{abstract}
